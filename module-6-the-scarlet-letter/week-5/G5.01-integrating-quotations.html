📖 FOCUS
A quotation does not explain itself. A sentence has to introduce it, fit it, and cite it.
Short quotations in a literary essay are part of the writer’s sentence, not separate from it. This lesson covers blending, punctuating, cutting, and citing quotations from The Scarlet Letter in MLA style.
🎯 OBJECTIVES
1
I can blend a quotation into my own sentence with correct punctuation.
2
I can cite a quotation from a novel in MLA with the author and chapter.
3
I can mark an omission with an ellipsis.

📚 PART 2 · THE LESSON

A literary essay proves its claim with the novel’s own words, and each quotation needs a frame. Integrating a quotation means working it into the grammar of the writer’s sentence, citing it, and saying what it shows. MLA citation for a novel read online uses the author’s last name and the chapter number in parentheses, with the sentence’s end punctuation placed after the citation. When the sentence already names Hawthorne, the citation is (ch. 24).

FOUR MOVES, IN ORDER
Introduce the moment in your own words. Quote only the words that carry the point. Cite (Hawthorne, ch. N). Explain what the words show about your claim. A quotation without the fourth move is evidence with no argument.
ON THE BOARD · QUOTING THE NOVEL IN MLA
WRONGHester will not name the father. “It is too deeply branded.” (Hawthorne, ch. 3)
RIGHTHester tells the ministers the letter is “too deeply branded” to be taken off (Hawthorne, ch. 3).
A quotation never stands as its own sentence. Fold it into yours, then cite; the period follows the citation.
WRONGHawthorne writes that: “The letter was the symbol of her calling” (Hawthorne, ch. 13).
RIGHTHawthorne writes, “The letter was the symbol of her calling” (Hawthorne, ch. 13).
No colon after that. After a signal verb like writes, a comma introduces a full-sentence quotation. A colon follows only a complete clause: Hawthorne gives the letter a new name in Chapter XIII: “They said that it meant Able” (Hawthorne, ch. 13).
WRONGBy the end, the letter has “ceased to be a stigma” (Hawthorne).
RIGHTBy the end, the letter has “ceased to be a stigma” (Hawthorne, ch. 24).
A novel has no stable page numbers online. Cite the author and the chapter: (Hawthorne, ch. 24).
WRONGHawthorne says the letter “ceased to be a stigma and became a type of something to be sorrowed over” (Hawthorne, ch. 24).
RIGHTHawthorne says the letter “ceased to be a stigma . . . and became a type of something to be sorrowed over” (Hawthorne, ch. 24).
Words cut from inside a quotation are marked with an ellipsis: three spaced periods. Silent cuts misquote the author.
Every quotation here is copied from the chapter text. The citation follows the closing quotation mark, and the sentence’s period follows the citation.
