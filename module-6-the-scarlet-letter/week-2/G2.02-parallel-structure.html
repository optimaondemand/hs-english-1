Parallel Structure
Grammar
📖 FOCUS
A list can march or stumble. What makes the items in a series fit together?
Parallel structure gives matching ideas matching form. This lesson covers series and pairs, the correlative pairs, and how repetition builds a climax in Hawthorne's sentences.
📚 PART 1 · WARM-UP
💭 READ BOTH ALOUD
A. Chillingworth gathers herbs, questions his patient, and watching the minister's face.   |   B. Chillingworth gathers herbs, questions his patient, and watches the minister's face.
Which sentence reads without a stumble?
📚 PART 2 · THE LESSON

Parallel structure gives equal ideas equal grammatical form. A reader sees the pattern before understanding it, so a matched series sounds complete and a broken one sounds careless. Parallel form also carries meaning: when Bellingham lists three actions to be taken with Pearl, the repeated form makes the program sound thorough and unyielding. Repetition of an opening, as in Dimmesdale's imagined confession, adds one claim after another until the last phrase lands.

ONE RULE TO REMEMBER
Items in a series, a pair, or a comparison share one grammatical form: all nouns, all -ing phrases, all to phrases, or all clauses. After not only… but also, both… and, and either… or, the same form follows each half. Test: read the items one after another with the shared lead word. If any one sounds out of step, its form is different.
ON THE BOARD · PARALLEL STRUCTURE
IN HAWTHORNE · THREE KINDS OF PARALLELISM
“clad soberly, and disciplined strictly, and instructed in the truths of heaven and earth”
three participles, one form (Governor Bellingham, Chapter VIII)
“I, who have laid the hand of baptism upon your children”
“I, who have breathed the parting prayer over your dying friends”
one opening repeated to build a climax (Dimmesdale's imagined confession, Chapter XI)
“The elders, the deacons, the motherly dames, and the young and fair maidens”
four noun phrases in a series (Chapter IX)
WRONG, THEN RIGHT
✗ Chillingworth gathered herbs, studied his patient, and was watching the minister's face.
✓ Chillingworth gathered herbs, studied his patient, and watched the minister's face.
✗ Hester wanted to keep Pearl, to teach her, and for her to grow up safe.
✓ Hester wanted to keep Pearl, to teach her, and to see her grow up safe.
✗ Dimmesdale suffered not only from guilt but also the town's reverence hurt him.
✓ Dimmesdale suffered not only from guilt but also from the town's reverence.
The gold marks the matching parts. Each wrong sentence breaks the pattern at its last item: was watching after two simple past verbs, a clause after two to phrases, a clause after a from phrase.

📚 WORD STUDY
parallel structure: equal ideas given equal grammatical form  ·  series: three or more items in a row  ·  correlative conjunctions: paired words such as not only… but also
Word origins: Parallel comes from Greek para allelon, “beside one another.” Series comes from Latin series, “a row, a chain,” from serere, “to join.” Correlative combines Latin com-, “together,” and relativus, “referring”: the two words refer to each other.
