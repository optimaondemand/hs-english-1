Participial Phrases in The Scarlet Letter
Grammar · description that carries action
📖 FOCUS
Hawthorne often opens a sentence with a description that is also an action. What is that phrase, and how does a writer keep it attached to the right noun?
A participial phrase is built on a verb form used as an adjective: Preceded by the beadle, Hester Prynne set forth. This lesson reviews the form, then concentrates on punctuation and on the placement rule that prevents a dangling modifier.
🎯 OBJECTIVES
1
I can identify a participial phrase and the noun it modifies.
2
I can punctuate opening and interrupting participial phrases.
3
I can revise a sentence with a dangling modifier.

📚 PART 2 · THE FORM, THE COMMA, THE PLACEMENT

A participle is a verb form that works as an adjective. The present participle ends in -ing (stretching, knowing, touching, brooding). The past participle usually ends in -ed or -en, or is irregular (preceded, attended, involved). A participial phrase is the participle with its objects and modifiers. It describes a noun or pronoun, and it does not need a helping verb.

TWO RULES
Placement: the phrase modifies the noun right beside it. An opening phrase modifies the subject of the sentence. Commas: an opening phrase takes a comma after it; a phrase that interrupts takes a comma on each side; a phrase that closes the sentence takes a comma before it when it adds extra information.
ON THE BOARD · SENTENCES FROM THE NOVEL
Preceded by the beadle, and attended by an irregular procession of stern-browed men and unkindly visaged women, Hester Prynne set forth towards the place appointed for her punishment. (Ch. II · past participles; modifies Hester Prynne)
Stretching forth the official staff in his left hand, he laid his right upon the shoulder of a young woman… (Ch. II · present participle; modifies he, the beadle)
Involved in these thoughts, she scarcely heard a voice behind her… (Ch. III · past participle; modifies she)
Brooding over all these matters, the mother felt like one who has evoked a spirit… (Ch. VI · present participle; modifies the mother)
Then, touching the shoulder of a townsman who stood next to him, he addressed him, in a formal and courteous manner. (Ch. III · opening position, after Then; modifies he)
WRONG, THEN RIGHT · ORIGINAL SENTENCES
✗ Standing on the scaffold, the crowd stared at Hester.
✓ Standing on the scaffold, Hester faced the crowd.
The crowd is not the one standing on the scaffold; the phrase dangles.
✗ Holding her child, the scaffold seemed unbearable to Hester.
✓ Holding her child, Hester found the scaffold almost unbearable.
The scaffold cannot hold a child; the subject must be Hester.
✗ Knowing her part Hester climbed the steps.
✓ Knowing her part, Hester climbed the steps.
An opening participial phrase needs its comma.
The gold underline marks each participial phrase. In every correct sentence the phrase sits beside the noun it describes.

💪 THE ONE THING TO CARRY FORWARD

A participial phrase describes the noun beside it. At the front of a sentence it describes the subject; when the subject is the wrong noun, the modifier dangles.
