Semicolons in Balanced Sentences
Grammar · two ideas, one sentence, equal weight
📖 FOCUS
Hawthorne often sets two ideas side by side and lets the punctuation mark the balance. Which mark does that work?
A semicolon joins two closely related independent clauses without a conjunction. This lesson covers three uses (the balanced pair, the series of parallel clauses, and the semicolon before a conjunctive adverb) and the errors that go with each.
🎯 OBJECTIVES
1
I can use a semicolon to join two related independent clauses.
2
I can write a balanced sentence whose two halves share a pattern.
3
I can punctuate a conjunctive adverb after a semicolon.
📋 MATERIALS
▶
Example sentences from Chapter IV, Chapter V, and Chapter VI of The Scarlet Letter (public domain)
▶
About 20 minutes
📚 PART 1 · WARM-UP
💭 WARM-UP
A. The law condemned her. The crowd condemned her.   |   B. The law condemned her; the crowd condemned her.
Which version makes the two condemnations feel equal in weight?
📚 PART 2 · THE SEMICOLON AT WORK

Each side of a semicolon must be able to stand as a complete sentence. A balanced sentence goes further: the two halves share a grammatical pattern, so the semicolon signals a match or a contrast. A semicolon also separates items in a series when the items already contain commas, and it comes before a conjunctive adverb (however, therefore, nevertheless) that joins two independent clauses.

ONE TEST
Cover one side of the semicolon. If the other side cannot stand alone as a sentence, the semicolon is wrong. Never place one before because, although, if, or when, and a plain and or but after it is almost always unnecessary.
ON THE BOARD · SENTENCES FROM THE NOVEL
The child had a native grace which does not invariably coexist with faultless beauty; its attire, however simple, always impressed the beholder as if it were the very garb that precisely became it best. (Ch. VI · two related statements about the child)
Her needlework was seen on the ruff of the Governor; military men wore it on their scarfs, and the minister on his band; it decked the baby’s little cap; it was shut up, to be mildewed and moulder away, in the coffins of the dead. (Ch. V · series of independent clauses, one for each place the needlework appears)
WRONG, THEN RIGHT · ORIGINAL SENTENCES
✗ Hester stood on the scaffold, Dimmesdale watched from the balcony.
✓ Hester stood on the scaffold; Dimmesdale watched from the balcony.
A comma alone cannot join two independent clauses (a comma splice).
✗ The crowd wanted a harsher sentence; because they believed the law required it.
✓ The crowd wanted a harsher sentence because they believed the law required it.
A semicolon cannot come before a dependent clause.
✗ Hester refused to speak; however she kept her gaze on the minister.
✓ Hester refused to speak; however, she kept her gaze on the minister.
A conjunctive adverb after a semicolon takes a comma.
Each ; is highlighted. Cover either half of a sentence: the other half still stands alone.
✎ YOUR TURN · CHOOSE THE BEST ANSWER
1. Which sentence uses the semicolon correctly?
2. Which revision fixes the comma splice The beadle opened a lane, the crowd stepped back?
3. Which sentence punctuates the conjunctive adverb correctly?
➡️ NOW WRITE
Write two balanced sentences about Hester and the crowd, each joined by a semicolon, with the two halves in matching patterns. Then write one sentence that uses a semicolon before however or therefore, with the comma after it.
➡️ BEFORE THE NEXT LESSON
Read Chapters IV to VI of The Scarlet Letter: Chapter IV, Chapter V, Chapter VI.
💪 THE ONE THING TO CARRY FORWARD

A semicolon joins two independent clauses that belong together. When the halves also match in structure, the sentence is balanced and the punctuation shows it.
