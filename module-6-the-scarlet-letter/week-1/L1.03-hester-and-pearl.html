Hester at Her Needle; Pearl (Ch. IV–VI)
Reading lesson · the interview, the long sentence, and the child
📖 FOCUS
Hester is free to leave Boston, and she stays. Chillingworth says he wants no vengeance, and her child is named Pearl. What holds Hester after the scaffold?
Chapters IV to VI move from one public scene to the long daily sentence. This lesson follows three bonds that hold Hester in place: the community’s mark, Chillingworth’s oath, and Pearl.

📚 PART 2 · THE INTERVIEW, THE NEEDLE, THE CHILD
THREE BONDS THAT HOLD HESTER IN PLACE
HESTER PRYNNE
▼
THE MARK
The town
“a mark of shame upon her bosom”
A sentence for life. Hester becomes “the general symbol at which the preacher and moralist might point”.
THE OATH
Chillingworth
“Swear it!”
Hester promises not to reveal that he was her husband. He vows to find her partner: “Not the less he is mine”.
THE CHILD
Pearl
“the scarlet letter on Hester’s bosom”
The first object Pearl notices in her life. The child is a second mark, one that grows and speaks.
Chapters IV to VI replace one public scene with three continuing pressures.

The interview (Chapter IV)

Chillingworth treats Pearl first, then Hester. He claims no desire for revenge: “Between thee and me the scale hangs fairly balanced.” He shares the blame: “It was my folly, and thy weakness.” Then he turns to the man Hester will not name: “I shall seek this man, as I have sought truth in books; as I have sought gold in alchemy.” He demands one thing in return: “Breathe not, to any human soul, that thou didst ever call me husband!” Hester takes the oath.

Hawthorne sets his acts against his words. Hester sees the gap at once: “Thy acts are like mercy”, she says, “But thy words interpret thee as a terror!” Chillingworth’s calm language conceals a purpose, and the oath makes Hester complicit in the concealment.

The needle (Chapter V)

Hester lives at the edge of town and supports herself and her child by needlework. The same colony that shames her buys her work: it appears on the Governor’s ruff, on soldiers’ scarves, on the minister’s band, and on baby caps and funeral garments. One exception stands out: “it is not recorded that, in a single instance, her skill was called in aid to embroider the white veil which was to cover the pure blushes of a bride”. The narrator draws the conclusion: “The exception indicated the ever-relentless rigor with which society frowned upon her sin.”

Needlework is also where Hester’s art meets her penance. She makes plain garments for the poor and keeps for herself only the letter. Of her pleasure in fine work, the narrator says “Like all other joys, she rejected it as sin.” Under the daily gaze, she feels “the scarlet letter had endowed her with a new sense”, a doubtful, troubling power to sense hidden sin in others.

The child (Chapter VI)

Hester names her daughter Pearl because the child was “of great price”, bought with everything Hester had. The narrator sets the child’s origin against the letter: “Man had marked this woman’s sin by a scarlet letter”, and “God, as a direct consequence of the sin which man thus punished, had given her a lovely child”. Two marks, one from the community and one from a source Hester cannot name, now lie on the same breast.

Pearl is described in opposite ways. She is “worthy to have been brought forth in Eden”, and the settlement’s view is harsher: “An imp of evil, emblem and product of sin”. In the flower scene she throws wild-flowers at the letter, and Hester asks “Child, what art thou?” Pearl answers “O, I am your little Pearl!” When Hester says Pearl’s Heavenly Father sent her, Pearl touches the letter and cries “I have no Heavenly Father!”.

📚 WORD STUDY
penance: an act of self-punishment that shows sorrow for wrongdoing.  ·  alchemy: the medieval forerunner of chemistry, which sought to turn base metals into gold.  ·  ascetic: practicing strict self-denial.  ·  emblem: an object that stands for an idea.
Word origins: Penance comes from Latin paenitentia, “regret.” Alchemy came into English through medieval Latin from Arabic al-kimiya. Ascetic comes from Greek askesis, “training.” Emblem comes from Greek emblema, “something inlaid.”
💪 THE ONE THING TO CARRY FORWARD

After the scaffold, three bonds hold Hester: the town’s mark, Chillingworth’s oath, and Pearl. Each one tests what she owes to the community, to the truth, and to the child.
