Hawthorne and Puritan Boston
Background · the colony, the writer, and the distance between them
📖 FOCUS
A Boston crowd gathers to watch a woman judged in public. What did that crowd believe about sin, law, and community?
The Scarlet Letter was published in 1850 and is set in Boston in the 1640s. This lesson covers how Puritan Boston joined law and religion, how it punished in public, and where Hawthorne stands as a writer looking back two centuries.

📚 PART 1 · THE COLONY
💭 CHECK-IN
A community writes its moral rules into law, and a member breaks one. Which response seems most natural?

The colony

Puritans were English Protestants who wanted to purify the Church of England. Groups of them founded the Massachusetts Bay Colony in 1630 and settled Boston. They aimed to organize a whole society around obedience to God as they read Scripture, and in the colony church membership shaped who held a voice in public life.

Hawthorne describes the crowd in Chapter II as “a people amongst whom religion and law were almost identical”. Ministers, magistrates, and the governor stand together on one balcony because, for them, a sin was also a crime. Public punishment was a teaching method: the same chapter lists a bond-servant corrected at the whipping-post, a religious dissenter “scourged out of the town”, and a witch sent to the gallows.

The history behind those lines is real. Anne Hutchinson was banished in the late 1630s for teaching her own religious views. Quakers were whipped and banished, and between 1659 and 1661 four were hanged in Boston. Shaming punishments were common in colonial New England, and adultery was a serious crime under colony law.

📚 PART 2 · THE WRITER AND THE DISTANCE

The writer

Nathaniel Hawthorne was born in Salem, Massachusetts, in 1804. His ancestors were early Puritan settlers, and one of them, John Hathorne, served as a judge at the 1692 Salem witch trials. Hawthorne changed the spelling of the family name. He wrote about Puritan judgment as a descendant of the judges.

The novel appeared about two centuries after its events, and its narrator says so: the opening scene takes place “not less than two centuries ago”. That distance lets Hawthorne describe the Puritans with some admiration and examine them with some doubt at the same time.

TIMELINE · FROM THE COLONY TO THE NOVEL
1630
Puritans settle Boston and found the Massachusetts Bay Colony.
1637–38
Anne Hutchinson is tried and banished for her religious views. Chapter I names her.
c. 1645–50
The novel’s present: Boston, fifteen to twenty years after settlement. Real figures appear: Governor Bellingham and the Reverend John Wilson.
1692
The Salem witch trials. Hawthorne’s ancestor John Hathorne is one of the judges.
1804
Nathaniel Hawthorne is born in Salem, Massachusetts.
1850
The Scarlet Letter is published, about two centuries after the events it imagines.
Gold marks the two dates that frame the reading: the world the novel describes and the year Hawthorne wrote it.
THE NARRATOR’S DOUBLE VIEW · CHAPTER III
Of the magistrates: “They were, doubtless, good men, just and sage.” Then the qualification: they were “less capable of sitting in judgment on an erring woman’s heart” than almost any group that could have been chosen. Praise first, doubt second: the same pattern recurs through the novel.

📚 WORD STUDY
Puritan: a member of the English Protestant movement that sought to purify the Church of England.  ·  theocracy: government in which religious leaders and religious law hold civil power.  ·  ignominy: public shame and disgrace.
Word origins: Puritan comes from purify, the goal of the movement. Theocracy joins Greek theos, “god,” and kratos, “rule.” Ignominy comes from Latin ignominia: in-, “not,” and nomen, “name,” the loss of one’s good name.

💪 THE ONE THING TO CARRY FORWARD

Puritan Boston treated sin as a public and legal matter, and Hawthorne writes about it from two centuries away. Every scene of judgment in the novel carries both views at once.
