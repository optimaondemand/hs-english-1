WEEK 4 · LITERATURE
The Revelation and the Conclusion (Ch. XXIII–XXIV)
Lesson · Chapters XXIII and XXIV
🎯 FOCUS
Dimmesdale confesses on the scaffold and dies. Does the confession settle what his life meant, and what does Hawthorne leave for the reader to decide?
Chapter XXIII brings Dimmesdale, Hester, Pearl, and Chillingworth to the scaffold before the whole town. Chapter XXIV reports what the town made of it, closes each character’s story, and states the novel’s moral in one sentence.
🎯 OBJECTIVES
1
I can narrate the sequence of Dimmesdale’s confession and name what each witness does.
2
I can compare the accounts of the scaffold scene that Chapter XXIV reports and describe the narrator’s stance toward each.
3
I can state how the meaning of the scarlet letter has changed by the end of the novel.

📚 PART 1 · THE SCAFFOLD (CHAPTER XXIII)
💭 WARM-UP
A respected leader admits a long-hidden wrong in front of the whole community. What happens next?

After the Election Sermon the crowd praises Dimmesdale as never before; Hawthorne places him on “the very proudest eminence of superiority”. In the procession he is pale and unsteady. He refuses the offered arm of John Wilson, stops at the scaffold, and calls out: “come hither! Come, my little Pearl!”

Chillingworth pushes through the crowd: “Madman, hold! what is your purpose?” Dimmesdale answers: “Ha, tempter! Methinks thou art too late!” Supported by Hester, he climbs the scaffold and addresses the town: “behold me here, the one sinner of the world!” He says that Hester’s letter is only “the shadow of what he bears on his own breast”. He tears away the band over his chest, and Hawthorne declines to describe what the crowd sees: “It was revealed! But it were irreverent to describe that revelation.”

Dimmesdale asks Pearl, “wilt thou kiss me now?” She does, and Hawthorne writes “A spell was broken.” Chillingworth repeats “Thou hast escaped me!” Dimmesdale answers “Thou, too, hast deeply sinned!” When Hester asks whether they will meet again, he tells her to think of the law they broke and the sin now revealed. He dies after giving thanks for God’s mercy.

📚 PART 2 · THE CONCLUSION (CHAPTER XXIV)

Chapter XXIV closes the story in four moves. First, the competing accounts of the scaffold scene (below). Second, the narrator’s moral, set down as the one sentence he will put on the whole experience: “Be true! Be true! Be true! Show freely to the world, if not your worst, yet some trait whereby the worst may be inferred!”

WHAT WAS SEEN ON THE SCAFFOLD · THE ACCOUNTS IN CHAPTER XXIV
ACCOUNT 1
Penance
A mark Dimmesdale inflicted on himself, beginning the day Hester first wore her badge.
ACCOUNT 2
Magic
A mark Chillingworth caused by magic and poisonous drugs.
ACCOUNT 3
Remorse
The effect of “the ever-active tooth of remorse, gnawing from the inmost heart outwardly”.
ACCOUNT 4
No mark
Witnesses who saw no mark. For them his death was a parable that “we are sinners all alike”.
Of the first three, the narrator says: “The reader may choose among these theories.” He treats the fourth as an example of friends’ loyalty to a reputation.

Third, the fates of the others. Chillingworth, who had made revenge the principle of his life, withers “like an uprooted weed that lies wilting in the sun”. The narrator wonders “whether hatred and love be not the same thing at bottom.” Chillingworth leaves his property to Pearl, who becomes the richest heiress of her day in the New World.

Fourth, Hester. She returns to the cottage and resumes the letter “of her own free will”. Over the years the letter changes in the town’s eyes: “the scarlet letter ceased to be a stigma which attracted the world’s scorn and bitterness, and became a type of something to be sorrowed over, and looked upon with awe, yet with reverence too.” The novel ends on a tombstone motto, “ON A FIELD, SABLE, THE LETTER A, GULES.” In heraldry sable is black and gules is red: a red letter A on a black field.

✎ CHECK YOURSELF · PICK THE BEST ANSWER
1. Why does Chapter XXIV show Chillingworth withering soon after Dimmesdale dies?
2. What does the narrator mean by “The reader may choose among these theories.”
3. Why does Hester return to New England and put the letter on again?
