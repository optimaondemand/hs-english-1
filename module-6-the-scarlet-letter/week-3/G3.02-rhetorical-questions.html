GRAMMAR LESSON
Rhetorical Questions
Questions asked to persuade, and how to punctuate every kind
📖 FOCUS
Hester asks, “Is the world, then, so narrow?” and does not wait for a reply. What is the question doing?
A rhetorical question carries its own answer. Hawthorne’s characters use them to argue, accuse, and defend, and the forest chapters are full of them. This lesson separates them from genuine questions and covers the punctuation of direct and indirect questions.
🎯 OBJECTIVES
1
I can tell a rhetorical question from a genuine one.
2
I can explain what a rhetorical question does in an argument.
3
I can punctuate direct and indirect questions correctly.
📚 PART 1 · WARM-UP
💭 WARM-UP
Both are questions from Chapter XVII. A. “Who speaks?”  |  B. “Is the world, then, so narrow?” Which one expects an answer?
📚 PART 2 · THE LESSON

A rhetorical question is asked for effect, not information. The answer is already contained in the question, so the speaker does not wait for one. It argues by making the listener supply the conclusion. A genuine question asks for something the speaker does not know.

Test: would the speaker be satisfied, or surprised, by an answer? If the answer is obvious and the speaker is pressing a point, the question is rhetorical.

Hawthorne’s characters use rhetorical questions to argue, to accuse, and to defend. Hester argues with them in Chapter XVII, Chillingworth defends himself with one in Chapter XIV, and Dimmesdale turns one against himself.

PUNCTUATION
A direct question, quoted or asked, ends in a question mark. An indirect question, reported inside a statement (asked whether, wondered if), ends in a period.
ON THE BOARD · ASKED TO KNOW, OR ASKED TO PERSUADE
GENUINE: THE SPEAKER WANTS TO KNOW
“Who speaks?”  (XVII, Dimmesdale)
“And who told you this story, Pearl?”  (XVI, Hester)
RHETORICAL: THE ANSWER IS BUILT IN
“Is the world, then, so narrow?”  (XVII, Hester: no)
“Hast thou not tortured him enough?”  (XIV, Hester: yes)
“What choice had you?”  (XIV, Chillingworth: none)
“What can a ruined soul, like mine, effect towards the redemption of other souls?”  (XVII, Dimmesdale: nothing)
WRONG, THEN RIGHT
Hester asked whether the world was so narrow?  →  Hester asked whether the world was so narrow.
“Is the world so narrow.” asked Hester.  →  “Is the world so narrow?” asked Hester.

✎ YOUR TURN · CHOOSE THE BEST ANSWER
1. Which of these is a rhetorical question?
2. What does Hester’s “Is the world, then, so narrow?” accomplish in her argument?
3. Which sentence is punctuated correctly?
➡️ PRACTICE

Write one rhetorical question that Hester could ask Dimmesdale about the town. Then restate it as an indirect question with the correct end punctuation.

💪 KEY POINT

A rhetorical question asks for no answer because it already holds one. A direct question ends in a question mark; an indirect question ends in a period.
