WRITING LESSON
Building the Cross-Text Argument
Lesson · thesis, outline, and the researched source
📖 FOCUS
Evidence from three texts does not argue anything until a thesis says what it shows. How does a chart become a claim and a claim become an outline?
This lesson moves from the Feeder 2 chart to a thesis, then to an outline organized by idea. The researched source is placed where it changes a reading.
🎯 OBJECTIVES
1
I can write an arguable thesis that names three texts.
2
I can outline body paragraphs by idea, not by text.
3
I can place the researched source so that it does work.

📚 PART 1 · FROM EVIDENCE TO THESIS
💭 WARM-UP
A draft thesis reads: Loyalty is important in Electra, Sir Gawain, and The Crucible. What is missing?

A thesis answers the research question from Feeder 1 and rests on the evidence chart from Feeder 2. It moves up three rungs.

RungExampleStatus
TopicLoyalty in Electra, Sir Gawain, and The Crucible.A label. Nothing to argue.
ObservationCharacters in all three texts protect someone they are loyal to.True, and not disputable.
ThesisElectra, Sir Gawain and the Green Knight, and The Crucible each show a person who protects someone at the truth’s expense, and each text judges that protection by what it later costs the protector.Arguable, names all three texts, takes a position.
THREE TESTS FOR A THESIS
Could a reader disagree? Do all three texts help prove it? Does it answer the research question?
📚 PART 2 · THE OUTLINE
THE CROSS-TEXT ESSAY · 900–1,200 WORDS
INTRODUCTION · 120–150 words
Theme, the three texts, the research question, and the thesis as the last sentence.
BODY 1 · 200–260 words
Idea one. Topic sentence, a moment from two or three texts, the point where they differ.
BODY 2 · 200–260 words
Idea two. The researched source changes how a moment is read.
BODY 3 · 200–260 words
Idea three. The strongest evidence, closest to the thesis.
COUNTERCLAIM · 100–150 words
The strongest objection to the thesis, stated fairly, then the reply.
CONCLUSION · 80–120 words
What follows if the thesis is right.
Body paragraphs are organized by idea, not by text. Every body paragraph holds evidence from at least two texts.

The cover test

Cover one text. If a body paragraph still makes its point, the paragraph is about one text and mentions the other. Revise until the covered paragraph fails.

Three jobs for the researched source

JobWhat the source does
ContextSupplies a fact about Salem in 1692 or the 1950s hearings that changes how a moment reads.
A claim to testOffers a critic’s reading that the texts support, complicate, or contradict.
An objectionSupplies the strongest counterclaim, which the thesis then answers.
