LESSON · ACT FOUR
Act Four: Proctor’s Choice
Lesson · Act Four
📖 FOCUS
Proctor can save his life by signing a sheet of paper. Why does he not?
Act Four moves to a Salem jail on an autumn morning, some time after Act Three. The court is losing its hold, and the question shifts from what is true to what a person will sign. Proctor’s choice gathers justice, loyalty, and conscience into one object: his name.

📚 PART 1 · THE JAIL
💭 WARM-UP
Someone in authority offers safety in exchange for one thing. Which would be hardest to give up?

What the court wants

Parris reports that Abigail has fled with his savings and that Andover has rejected its own court. Parris and Hale ask Danforth to postpone the hangings. Danforth refuses, because postponing would suggest the earlier hangings were wrong. Hale, who quit the court in Act Three, now urges the condemned to confess, because he values their lives over their pride. Elizabeth, who is pregnant, is brought to Proctor in the hope that she will persuade him.

📚 PART 2 · THREE DECISIONS AND A NAME

Proctor’s decisions come in order, and each one narrows the question. First he confesses, which is a lie told to live. Then he is asked to accuse others, and he stops. Last, the court wants his signed confession posted where the town can read it, and he stops again.

PROCTOR’S THREE DECISIONS IN ACT FOUR
1
CONFESS?
THE DEMAND
The court offers his life in exchange for a confession to witchcraft.
HIS CHOICE
After speaking with Elizabeth, Proctor agrees to confess, though he knows the charge is false.
2
NAME OTHERS?
THE DEMAND
Danforth asks for the names of other people seen with the devil.
HIS CHOICE
Proctor refuses. He will speak of his own sins and will not accuse anyone else.
3
LET THE PAPER BE POSTED?
THE DEMAND
Danforth wants the signed confession kept and displayed in public.
HIS CHOICE
Proctor signs, then takes the paper back and tears it rather than let his name be used.
He gives up the truth to save his life, then refuses to give up his name.

One word, three meanings: name

In Act Four name means reputation, what the town thinks of him; signature, the writing that makes a confession official; and self, the person he is when no one is watching. The play lets him give up the first and keep the third. The signature is where the two meet.

Two refusals to name names

HESTER PRYNNE · THE SCARLET LETTER, CHAPTER III
“I will not speak!” answered Hester, turning pale as death, but responding to this voice, which she too surely recognized. “And my child must seek a heavenly Father; she shall never know an earthly one!”
JOHN PROCTOR · THE CRUCIBLE, ACT FOUR
Danforth demands the names of others. Proctor refuses, and he refuses again when the court wants his signed confession displayed.

Both characters answer an authority that demands a name, and both refuse. Hester refuses in public, with the whole town watching, to protect the minister who stands on the balcony above her. Proctor refuses in a jail, before a judge, because he will not make himself someone else’s accuser. Hester’s refusal leaves her alive and under judgment. Proctor’s refusal ends in the gallows.

History and the play

The historical John Proctor was hanged in August 1692. Elizabeth Proctor was pregnant and was not executed. Giles Corey was pressed to death in September 1692 for refusing to enter a plea; in the play, Act Four reports his death. Miller changed facts for the stage: the historical Abigail Williams was about eleven or twelve years old, and the affair is Miller’s invention.

📚 WORD STUDY
integrity: the state of being whole and undivided in one’s principles.  ·  conscience: the inner sense of right and wrong.  ·  martyr: a person who suffers death rather than give up a belief.  ·  recant: to take back a statement formally.
Word origins: integrity comes from Latin integer, “whole.” Conscience comes from Latin conscientia, “knowing with oneself.” Martyr comes from Greek martys, “witness.” Recant comes from Latin recantare, “to revoke.” Write one sentence about Proctor that uses integrity and recant.
💪 THE ONE THING TO CARRY FORWARD

Proctor is willing to lie to live and unwilling to let the lie carry his name. The test of the act is the difference between what he will say and what he will sign.
