Subordination and Concession
Grammar · clauses that admit the other side
📖 FOCUS
A strong argument grants what is true in the other view before it makes its own claim.
A concession clause begins with although, though, even though, while, or whereas. It admits a fact that seems to work against the writer, and the main clause then makes the claim. Used well, it shows the reader that the other side has been weighed.

📚 PART 2 · THE CONCESSION CLAUSE

A concession clause is subordinate: it has its own subject and verb, but the opening word makes it depend on a main clause. Although the court claimed to seek the truth cannot stand alone. The main clause supplies the claim: it silenced the people who brought it. The pair reads as one move: here is what is true on the other side; here is why my claim still holds.

TWO RULES
1. A concession clause that opens the sentence is followed by a comma. At the end of the sentence the comma is optional; whereas usually takes one.
2. One connector does the work. Although never pairs with but in the same sentence.
ON THE BOARD · WRONG, THEN RIGHT
Although the court claimed to seek the truth.
Although the court claimed to seek the truth, it jailed Giles Corey for refusing to name a source.
Although Mary Warren told the truth, but the court did not believe her.
Although Mary Warren told the truth, the court did not believe her.
Hester kept silent though the whole town demanded a name.
Although Elizabeth Proctor is known for never lying, her one lie decides her husband’s fate.
Struck-through lines are errors: a fragment, then a doubled connector. Gold underline marks the concession clause. The comma follows a concession clause that comes first.

💪 THE ONE THING TO CARRY FORWARD

A concession clause admits the strongest point on the other side and then claims more. One connector, one comma, and a main clause that carries the claim.
