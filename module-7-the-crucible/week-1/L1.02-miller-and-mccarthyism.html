📚 PART 2 · THE PLAY AND ITS MOMENT

The Crucible opened in New York in January 1953. The setting is Salem in 1692, but the play was written during the early years of the Cold War, when many Americans feared communist influence at home. In 1947 the House Un-American Activities Committee (HUAC) opened hearings into communist influence in the film industry. In 1950 Senator Joseph McCarthy claimed that communists had entered the federal government. The term McCarthyism came to mean making accusations of disloyalty with little or no evidence.

Witnesses called before these committees were pressed to name other people. Those who refused could be blacklisted, meaning shut out of work in their industry, or charged with contempt of Congress. Miller himself was called before HUAC in 1956, declined to name others, and was convicted of contempt; the conviction was overturned on appeal.

Allegory is a work in which characters and events carry a second meaning about another subject, often a moral or political one. The play works this way, but not point for point. Miller kept the pattern of the hunt and changed historical facts: the historical Abigail Williams was about eleven, John Proctor was about sixty, and the affair between them is Miller’s invention.

📚 WORD STUDY
allegory: a work whose events carry a second meaning about another subject  ·  blacklist: a list of people to be shut out of work or favor  ·  contempt: open disrespect, or defiance of a court or legislative body  ·  parallel: a likeness between two things
Word origins: Allegory comes from Greek allegoria, “speaking otherwise.” Contempt comes from Latin contemnere, “to despise.” Parallel comes from Greek parallelos, “beside one another.” Blacklist is plain English: a list written to exclude.
