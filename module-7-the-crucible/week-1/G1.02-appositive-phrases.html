GRAMMAR · SENTENCE STRUCTURE
Appositive Phrases
Grammar lesson
📖 FOCUS
How can one sentence carry two facts about the same person?
An appositive renames a noun and adds information without a second sentence. The lesson uses facts from Salem in 1692 as its examples.
🎯 OBJECTIVES
1
I can identify an appositive in a sentence.
2
I can punctuate essential and nonessential appositives.
📚 PART 1 · WARM-UP
💭 WARM-UP
A. Parris was the village minister. He feared for his position.  |  B. Parris, the village minister, feared for his position.
Which version is tighter?
📚 PART 2 · THE LESSON

An appositive is a noun or noun phrase placed beside another noun to rename or explain it. It packs a second fact into the same sentence without a second sentence.

TWO KINDS
Nonessential: adds extra information, so it takes a comma on each side: Samuel Parris, the village minister, feared for his position. Essential: identifies which one is meant, so it takes no commas: The playwright Arthur Miller wrote about 1692 in 1953.
ON THE BOARD · WRONG, THEN RIGHT
✗ Samuel Parris the village minister feared for his position.
✓ Samuel Parris, the village minister, feared for his position.
✗ Abigail Williams, Parris’s niece stands at the center of the accusations.
✓ Abigail Williams, Parris’s niece, stands at the center of the accusations.
✗ Tituba confessed under pressure, Parris’s enslaved servant.
✓ Tituba, Parris’s enslaved servant, confessed under pressure.
✗ Rebecca Nurse was a respected church member. She was accused in 1692.
✓ Rebecca Nurse, a respected church member, was accused in 1692.
The gold phrase is the appositive. It renames the noun beside it, and it sits inside a pair of commas.

An appositive can also open a sentence: A farmer who spoke plainly, John Proctor distrusted Reverend Parris. A comma follows the opening appositive.

✎ CHECK YOURSELF · PICK THE BEST ANSWER
1. Which words are the appositive? Abigail Williams, Parris’s niece, led the girls into the forest.
2. Which sentence is punctuated correctly?
3. Combine into one sentence: Thomas Putnam was a wealthy landowner. He pressed several accusations.
➡️ PRACTICE

Write two sentences about people in 1692 Salem. Use a nonessential appositive in each, one in the middle of the sentence and one at the start.

💪 KEY IDEA

An appositive renames a noun. If it only adds information, enclose it in commas; if it identifies which noun is meant, use no commas.
