LESSON · HISTORICAL BACKGROUND
Salem, 1692: Theocracy and Accusation
Background lesson
📖 FOCUS
How did a village of farmers, a church, and a court turn a children’s illness into twenty executions?
Salem Village in 1692 was a Puritan community where church and civil law reinforced each other. This lesson sets out the historical facts that Arthur Miller built on, and changed, in The Crucible.

📚 PART 2 · A VILLAGE, A CHURCH, A COURT

Salem Village in 1692 belonged to the Massachusetts Bay Colony, founded by Puritans who believed that a community should be governed by the standards of scripture. In practice the colony worked as a theocracy: church membership carried civic weight, ministers advised magistrates, and the colony’s laws made witchcraft a crime punishable by death. Sin and crime were treated as one offense, and the Devil was understood as an active enemy.

The village was also divided. Salem Village sat apart from the wealthier Salem Town, and neighbors quarreled over land boundaries and over the pay of the minister, Samuel Parris. Those old grievances shaped who was accused and who accused.

TIMELINE · SALEM, 1691–1693
WINTER 1691–92
Betty Parris and Abigail Williams, the minister’s daughter and niece, begin to suffer fits. A physician concludes the cause is supernatural. Other girls in the village show the same symptoms.
LATE FEB–MARCH 1692
Tituba, Sarah Good, and Sarah Osborne are accused, arrested, and examined. Tituba confesses and describes a network of witches.
MAY 1692
Governor William Phips creates a special Court of Oyer and Terminer to hear the cases.
JUNE–SEPTEMBER 1692
Bridget Bishop is hanged in June; more hangings follow. In September Giles Corey is pressed to death for refusing to enter a plea.
OCTOBER 1692–1693
Phips ends the special court. Later trials reject spectral evidence, most defendants are acquitted, and the remaining prisoners are released.
Result: more than 200 people accused; nineteen hanged, one pressed to death, and at least five more dead in jail.
📚 PART 3 · HOW AN ACCUSATION BECAME A CONVICTION

The court relied heavily on spectral evidence: testimony that the specter, or spirit shape, of an accused person had appeared to the afflicted in a vision. No one else could see the specter, so no one could check it, and the accused could not disprove it.

The court also created a pattern of rewards. People who confessed and named others were generally spared; people who denied the charge were hanged. A confession therefore protected the confessor and widened the circle of suspects.

📚 WORD STUDY
theocracy: a society where religious belief and civil law are bound together  ·  spectral evidence: testimony that an accused person’s spirit appeared in a vision  ·  persecution: sustained harsh treatment of a person or group  ·  denounce: to accuse publicly
Word origins: Theocracy joins Greek theos, “god,” and kratos, “rule.” Spectral comes from Latin spectrum, “appearance, apparition.” Persecution comes from Latin persequi, “to pursue.” Denounce comes from Latin denuntiare, “to announce, to give warning.”
