Parallel Structure and Anaphora
Lesson
🎯 FOCUS
Equal ideas deserve equal grammar.
Parallel structure gives items in a series the same grammatical form. Anaphora repeats the opening words of successive clauses. Both make a list of events read as a pattern, and both are tools for argument as much as for drama. The examples below use events from Act Two.

📚 PART 2 · THE LESSON

A series is parallel when its items share a grammatical form: all nouns, all -ing phrases, all infinitive phrases, all clauses. The reader sorts the items as equals and moves faster. A mismatch signals that the writer did not finish the thought. The same applies to pairs joined by and, or, not only…but also.

Anaphora is repetition of the same word or phrase at the start of successive sentences or clauses. Where parallel structure makes items equal, anaphora makes them accumulate. The repeated opening marks a pattern, and the changing endings supply the evidence.

ONE RULE TO REMEMBER
Choose one form for every item in a series, then repeat the opening words only when the pattern itself is the point.
ON THE BOARD · WRONG, THEN RIGHT
PARALLEL STRUCTURE
WRONG  Hale asks about church attendance, to recite the commandments, and why a son is unbaptized.
RIGHT  Hale asks about church attendance, the commandments, and an unbaptized son.
PARALLEL STRUCTURE
WRONG  John wants to protect Elizabeth, his good name matters to him, and keeping the peace.
RIGHT  John wants to protect Elizabeth, to protect his name, and to keep the peace.
ANAPHORA
In the kitchen, a marriage strains. In the doorway, the court arrives. In the doll, a needle becomes evidence.
The gold underline marks the repeated opening. Each wrong version above breaks the form partway through the series.
