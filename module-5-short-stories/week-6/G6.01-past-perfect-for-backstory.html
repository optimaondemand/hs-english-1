Past Perfect for Backstory
Past perfect
🎯 FOCUS
Your story happens in the past tense. So how do you reach the past BEHIND that past: the inheritance, the old promise, the thing that happened before page one?
The past perfect: had + a past participle. It is the tense of backstory, the verb form that steps one layer deeper into the past and comes back without losing the reader. Maupassant leans on it whenever the Loisels' history has to enter the story's present: the francs Loisel had saved, the eighteen thousand his father had left him.
📚 PART 1 · WARM-UP
💭 READ BOTH ALOUD
A. She was exhausted because she worked all night.   |   B. She was exhausted because she had worked all night.
Which version makes the order of events unmistakable?
📚 PART 2 · THE LESSON

Most stories narrate in the simple past: she walked, he counted, they paid. That is the story's "now." The past perfect (had + past participle: had walked, had counted, had paid) reaches one step further back, to events already finished before the story's now. Watch Maupassant do it in one sentence: "Loisel possessed eighteen thousand francs which his father had left him." Possessed is the story's now; had left is the layer beneath it, the father's death and gift, delivered in two words without a scene. That is the tense's power: whole histories folded into a clause. The craft comes in the entering and the exiting. Enter backstory with a had or two to signal the step down; once the reader is anchored in the earlier time, you may relax into simple past; then return to the story's now cleanly, often with a time cue like now or this morning.

ONE RULE TO REMEMBER
Simple past = the story's now. Past perfect (had + past participle) = one step further back. Enter backstory with had; exit back to simple past once the history is told.
ON THE BOARD · SPOT THE STEP BACK
Loisel possessed eighteen thousand francs which his father had left him. (Maupassant)
She counted the coins again. She had defended them, sou by sou, all winter.
By the time the last note was paid, ten years had passed.
The exit move: …ten years had passed. Now she sat by the window and remembered the ball.
The gold underline marks the past perfect: had + past participle, always one layer earlier than the plain past verbs around it. Watch the last line's exit: one time cue ("Now") and a simple-past verb ("sat") bring the reader back to the story's now.

💪 KEY IDEA

One little had is the difference between a timeline and a muddle. Enter the deeper past with the past perfect, tell the history, and step back out. Whole inheritances fit in a clause when the tense does the filing.
