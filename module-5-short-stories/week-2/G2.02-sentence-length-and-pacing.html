Sentence Length and Pacing
Lesson
🎯 FOCUS
Readers do not just read your sentences. They breathe them. How do you control the breathing?
Sentence length is a pacing tool: long sentences to stretch time and gather dread, short ones to strike. This is the move that makes a suspense scene feel fast or slow on command.
📚 PART 1 · WARM-UP

Read the passage on the card aloud and count where you breathe. Note what happens in the last sentence. Then flip the card.

💭 READ ALOUD FIRST, THEN FLIP · NO SCORE
TAP TO FLIP AFTER YOU READ IT ALOUD
The wind worked at the shutters all evening, feeling along the frame like a stranger trying each window in turn, patient, unhurried, certain that one of them would give. The house held. Then the latch clicked.
The first sentence is 32 words: one long exhale that stretches time while dread gathers. Then two short strikes: three words, then four. Long builds, short hits. That rhythm is the whole lesson.
📚 PART 2 · THE LESSON

Pacing is the reader's sense of how fast a story moves, and sentence length is its throttle. A long sentence, built from stacked clauses and phrases, slows the reader down: the eye keeps moving, the breath keeps holding, and time inside the scene stretches. A short sentence does the opposite: it lands whole, in one beat, like a knock. Neither is better; the power is in the contrast. A paragraph of long sentences followed by a three-word sentence makes those three words hit with all the weight the long ones gathered. Jacobs paces his climax exactly this way: the long fumbling in the dark for the paw, and then the single sharp fact of the knocking. When every sentence is long, readers drown; when every sentence is short, the style turns to gunfire and loses its punch. Vary on purpose.

ONE RULE TO REMEMBER
Long sentences stretch time. Short sentences stop it. Spend the long ones to buy the short one its impact.
ON THE BOARD · ONE MOMENT, PACED TWO WAYS
FLAT: She heard a noise downstairs. She went to look. She saw the door was open. She was scared.

PACED: She stood at the top of the stairs listening to the house settle around her, telling herself it was the pipes, the wind, the old boards easing, anything but footsteps. The door stood open.
The flat version uses four same-length sentences: no rhythm, no dread. The paced version spends one long sentence stretching the moment, then strikes with four underlined words. Same events. Different heartbeat.

➡️ NOW WRITE YOUR OWN

Write a two-sentence suspense beat for your own story idea: first, one long sentence (25 words or more) that stretches the moment before something happens; then one short sentence (5 words or fewer) that lands the blow. Read both aloud to test the rhythm.

💪 KEY IDEA

Sentence length is rhythm, and rhythm is felt before it is understood. Build long, strike short.
