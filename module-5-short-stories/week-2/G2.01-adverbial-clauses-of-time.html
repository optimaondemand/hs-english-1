Adverbial Clauses of Time
Lesson
🎯 FOCUS
Suspense is a clock. How does a writer make the reader hear it ticking?
Clauses can answer the question when: clauses beginning with when, after, before, while, as, and until. Placed at the front of a sentence, they delay the main event for a heartbeat, and that delay is one of suspense writing's oldest tools.
📚 PART 1 · WARM-UP
💭 READ BOTH ALOUD
A. The knock came again.   |   B. Before she reached the stairs, the knock came again.
Which version makes you feel the knock is chasing her?
📚 PART 2 · THE LESSON

An adverbial clause of time is a subordinate clause that tells when the main action happens. It has its own subject and verb, but it begins with a time conjunction, when, after, before, while, as, until, so it cannot stand alone: After the footsteps faded… demands a completion. Suspense writers love these clauses at the front of the sentence for two reasons. First, the fronted clause forces the reader to hold their breath through the setup before the payoff lands. Second, it stitches two events onto one clock, so the scene feels like it is happening in real time. Jacobs paces his final scene this way: bolts scraping while the knocking swells, a wish breathed as the door begins to give.

ONE RULE TO REMEMBER
Time clause first → comma, then the main clause. Main clause first → usually no comma. The fronted clause is the suspense position.
ON THE BOARD · SPOT THE TIME CLAUSE
When the house went quiet, the first knock came.
After the candle guttered out, the old man felt for the paw in the dark.
She held the bolt until her fingers ached.
Before he could speak, the knocking began again.
The gold underline marks the time clause. Notice the comma appears only when the clause comes first, and notice how each fronted clause makes you wait for the main event.
✎ YOUR TURN · CHOOSE THE BEST ANSWER
1. Which part is the adverbial clause of time? While her husband searched the dark room, she kept her hand on the bolt.
2. Which sentence is punctuated correctly?
➡️ NOW WRITE YOUR OWN

Write a three-sentence suspense beat for a scene of your own invention (or your Story Seed idea): each sentence must open with a different time conjunction (when, after, before, while, as, or until), each with its comma in place. Make the third sentence's main clause the payoff.

💪 KEY IDEA

A fronted time clause is a held breath: it starts the clock, delays the payoff, and binds two events into one moment.
