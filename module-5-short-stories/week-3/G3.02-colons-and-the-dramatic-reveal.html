Colons and the Dramatic Reveal
Lesson
🎯 FOCUS
What punctuation mark makes a reader wait one beat before the door opens?
The colon. A colon stands after a complete sentence, announces what follows, and works as a drumroll: the pause that makes a reveal land harder.
📚 PART 1 · WARM-UP
💭 READ BOTH ALOUD
A. Behind the door waited a tiger.   |   B. Behind the door waited the one thing no one in the arena could tame: a tiger.
Which version makes you hold your breath for a half-second longer?
📚 PART 2 · THE LESSON

A colon stands at the end of a complete sentence and announces what comes next: a list, a definition, an explanation, or a reveal. The words before the colon must be able to stand alone as a sentence; the words after it deliver what was promised. That is the whole law, and it has one common violation: never put a colon directly after a verb like is or were, or after such as, because the sentence before the mark is not complete there. The two options were: a lady and a tiger breaks the rule. The arena offered two options: a lady and a tiger keeps it.

Grammar books call this "announcing." Storytellers call it a reveal. Everything before the colon raises the curtain; the pause is the drumroll; whatever follows steps into the light. Stockton understood this rhythm so well that he built an entire story shaped like a colon: a long announcement of a question, a pause, and then, where the reveal should be, his famous refusal.

ONE RULE TO REMEMBER
A colon follows a complete sentence and announces what comes next. If the words before the colon cannot stand alone, the colon cannot stand there.
ON THE BOARD · THE COLON AS DRUMROLL
The princess had bought one thing with her gold: the secret of the doors.
Two forces fought inside her: love and jealousy.
The king's justice came down to a single object: a door handle.
Cover everything after each gold colon with your hand and read the line aloud. Each opening stands alone as a full sentence: that is the test. Then lift your hand and feel the reveal land.
