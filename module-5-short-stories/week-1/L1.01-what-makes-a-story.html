The five parts of a plot

A plot is not just "the stuff that happens." It is a chain of cause and effect: each event happens because of the one before it. In the 1860s the German writer Gustav Freytag noticed that dramatic stories, from ancient Greek tragedy to the plays of his own day, tend to share one underlying shape, and writers have been sketching that shape ever since. It rises, peaks, and falls, in five parts.

A novel can climb this mountain slowly, over hundreds of pages. A short story sprints up it: "The Monkey's Paw" moves from a warm fireside to its climax in a few pages.
📚 PART 2 · CONFLICT, THE ENGINE OF PLOT

Conflict is the force of opposition that makes a story move. Without it there is no plot, only description: a character at breakfast is a scene; a character at breakfast reading a letter that ruins everything is a story. Writers usually name four main types of conflict, based on what the main character is up against.

Person vs. personA character struggles against another character: a rival, an enemy, a family member.
Person vs. selfThe struggle is inside: a choice, a temptation, a fear the character must face.
Person vs. natureA character struggles against storm, sea, wilderness, disease, or any force no one controls.
Person vs. societyA character struggles against rules, customs, or expectations larger than any one person.

Strong stories often layer two conflicts at once: an outer fight and an inner one. In "The Monkey's Paw," the White family's outer conflict (a magic paw that grants wishes) matters far less than their inner one (the temptation to use it).

📚 WORD STUDY
plot: the cause-and-effect chain of a story's events.  ·  conflict: the force of opposition that drives the story.  ·  exposition: the opening that establishes character, setting, and situation.  ·  climax: the turning point where the conflict is decided.  ·  resolution: the ending that establishes the new normal.
Word origins: conflict comes from Latin confligere, "to strike together": two forces colliding. Exposition comes from Latin exponere, "to set out for view." Climax is Greek for "ladder": the rungs a story climbs. Resolution comes from Latin resolvere, "to untie": the story's knot comes loose. Copia: take the sentence "The hero faced trouble," and rewrite it twice: once so the trouble is clearly a person vs. person conflict, and once so it is clearly person vs. self.
⚠️ WORTH KNOWING · AI AND PLOT SUMMARIES
An AI tool can label the five plot parts of a famous story in seconds, and it is usually accurate. What it cannot do is decide which moment of your story deserves to be the climax. That is a writer's choice.
