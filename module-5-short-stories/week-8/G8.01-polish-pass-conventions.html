The Polish Pass: Conventions for the Final Pass
Conventions
🎯 FOCUS
Your story is written. What do you fix in the last pass, and in what order?
This is not a new-rules lesson; it is a system for applying the rules already learned. Three sweeps run over a story, one error family at a time: dialogue punctuation, tense consistency, and pacing. Professionals proofread this way because hunting one error type at a time catches what a single read-through never will.

📚 PART 2 · THE LESSON: THREE SWEEPS, IN ORDER

Sweep 1: dialogue punctuation. Search your story for every quotation mark and check the joints. Commas and periods sit inside the closing quotation mark; a comma (not a period) joins the quote to its tag ("I know," she said); a new speaker always gets a new paragraph; and a question mark in the quote replaces the comma, with the tag still lowercase ("Where is it?" he asked). Sweep 2: tense consistency. Your story lives in one home tense, almost certainly past. Read only your verbs, start to finish; first drafts drift into present at high-excitement moments ("She grabs the rope") because you were excited writing them. Drift is a flaw; a flashback deliberately stepped into and out of is a choice. Know which one each shift is. Sweep 3: pacing. Read the story aloud, pencil in hand. Wherever your voice wants to hurry, the writing should already be hurrying: short sentences at high tension. Wherever you get bored reading aloud, so will your reader: cut or compress. And cut the throat-clearing everywhere, especially the warm-up sentences in front of paragraphs that start being good at sentence two.

ONE RULE TO REMEMBER
One sweep, one error family. Three targeted passes catch more in twenty minutes than five general read-throughs in an hour.
ON THE BOARD · THE THREE SWEEPS, EACH WITH A CATCH AND ITS FIX
SWEEP 1 · DIALOGUE JOINTS
✗ "I never wanted the money". He said.
✓ "I never wanted the money," he said.
SWEEP 2 · TENSE DRIFT
✗ She reached the door. Her hand shakes as she turns the key.
✓ She reached the door. Her hand shook as she turned the key.
SWEEP 3 · PACING
✗ It should also be noted that at that particular moment, the knocking, which had been going on for some time, suddenly stopped.
✓ The knocking stopped.
The ✗ lines show the three most common final-draft errors; the ✓ lines show each fix. Run the sweeps in this order: joints first, verbs second, ears last.

➡️ NOW RUN THE SWEEPS (ON YOUR REAL DRAFT)

Open your story draft with Track Changes on and run the three sweeps in order: every quotation joint, then every verb, then the read-aloud pacing pass. Keep three tally counts: joints fixed, drifts fixed, sentences cut. If all three tallies are zero, sweep again slower.

💪 KEY IDEA

Craftsmanship shows most in the last pass: clean dialogue joints, one steady tense, and prose that moves at the speed of its own suspense. Sweep three times so the surface matches the work underneath.
