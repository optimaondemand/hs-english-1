📚 PART 2 · THE ENDINGS GALLERY, AND WHAT "EARNED" MEANS

An ending is earned when the story has already paid for it: the details are planted, the character's choice is prepared, and the last paragraph spends what the earlier pages saved. An unearned ending arrives from outside, a twist with no clues, a rescue from nowhere, an "it was all a dream," and the reader feels cheated rather than moved. Study the gallery: seven stories, seven ending types, and for each one, the earlier move that paid for it.

THE ENDINGS GALLERY · SEVEN STORIES, SEVEN WAYS TO LAND A LAST PARAGRAPH
Story (week) Ending type The move at the end How the story earned it
The Monkey's Paw, Jacobs (Wk 2) Horror unseen The worst image is never shown: the door opens on "a quiet and deserted road." The reader's imagination does the final scene alone. Foreshadowing from the first page (the fatal chess mistake, the third wish "for death") trained the reader to dread; the withheld image cashes in that training.
The Lady, or the Tiger?, Stockton (Wk 3) Deliberately ambiguous The narrator stops at the door and hands the reader the question itself. The story refuses to end so the argument never can. Evidence for BOTH doors is planted in equal weight (the princess's love, her "semi-barbaric" blood, her hatred of the lady). Balanced planting is what makes the ambiguity fair rather than lazy.
The Cask of Amontillado, Poe (Wk 4) Vengeful confession Fifty years later, the murderer closes his boast with a blessing: "In pace requiescat!" The chill is that no justice ever arrives; the teller condemns himself out of his own mouth. The unreliable narrator was built for this: every early charm and courtesy doubled as evidence. The ending lands because we finally see the whole speech was a confession with no repentance in it.
An Occurrence at Owl Creek Bridge, Bierce (Wk 5) Twist The escape the reader has lived through collapses into a dying man's final second. One sentence rewrites the entire story behind you. The clues were always on the page: time stretching, senses sharpened past belief, the dreamlike wrongness of the escape. A twist is earned when a second reading finds it announced everywhere.
The Necklace, Maupassant (Wk 6) Ironic, cruel One line of dialogue reveals the necklace was paste, and ten years of sacrifice become a monument to a misunderstanding. The irony exposes what the longing was worth. Mathilde's vanity and the borrowed necklace's easy glamour were planted as the same illusion; the ending only prices it. The cruelty is fair because the character chose the illusion at every step.
The Machine Stops, Forster (Wk 7) Collapse, with a seed A whole civilization dies, but two people touch at last, and above the ruin the Homeless survive: "Humanity has learnt its lesson." The ending destroys the world to save the idea of one. The title promised the collapse; the small defects delivered it in installments; and Kuno's visit to the surface planted the survivors. Even an announced ending must be earned in stages.
The Gift of the Magi, O. Henry (Wk 8) Ironic, warm The twin ironies land (both gifts useless), and then the narrator reverses their meaning: "Of all who give and receive gifts, such as they are wisest." The device that could wound is aimed so it crowns. The two treasures were established with royal comparisons before either sacrifice; the warm, joking narrator taught us early how to hold the couple. Voice is planting too.
Read the last column top to bottom and one law emerges: every ending, dark, open, twisted, or warm, is paid for earlier, in planted details, balanced evidence, or a narrator's tone. The last paragraph only spends the money.

Now choose yours, out loud

A finished story needs a chosen ending: resolved, twist, or deliberately ambiguous, built so the story deserves it. The gallery gives you seven working models across those three families. Choosing means answering three questions in writing: (1) What should my reader feel in the last paragraph, in one word? (2) Which gallery ending produces the effect nearest to that, and by what move? (3) What has my draft already planted toward that move, and what must this week's revision add or cut? The answers become the revision plan.

✎ THINK, WRITE, THEN FLIP
CARD 1 · WRITE YOUR ANSWER, THEN FLIP
Bierce's twist and Stockton's ambiguity both withhold something from the reader. What does each withhold, and why is the effect so different?
Bierce withholds the truth (the escape never happened) and then delivers it, snapping the story shut behind you. Stockton withholds the outcome and never delivers it, leaving the story open forever. A twist ends the reader's work with a shock; ambiguity assigns the reader work that never ends.
CARD 2 · WRITE YOUR ANSWER, THEN FLIP
Maupassant and O. Henry both close on situational irony. Using the gallery's last column, explain why one ending is cruel and the other warm.
The planting differs. Maupassant plants an illusion (vanity, fake glamour), so the irony exposes and prices it: cruel, and earned. O. Henry plants two genuine loves and a joking, tender narrator, so the same device lands on the gifts instead of the givers: warm, and equally earned. Irony inherits its temperature from what was planted.
CARD 3 · WRITE YOUR ANSWER, THEN FLIP
A classmate's draft ends with the villain suddenly confessing, out of nowhere, on the last page. Using this lesson's terms, what would you tell them, and which two gallery stories would you send them to?
The ending is unearned: nothing planted pays it off, so it arrives from outside the story. Send them to Poe, whose confession IS the whole story and is prepared by every line of the narrator's charm, and to Bierce, whose shock survives rereading because the clues were planted throughout. Then have them list what their own draft must plant for the confession to be paid for.
🔧 THE EARNED-ENDING TEST (RUN IT ON YOUR DRAFT)
Read only your draft's first page and last page, skipping the middle. If the last page uses a person, object, or idea the first page never hints at, you have planting to do. If the first page plants something the last page never spends, you have paying off to do. When first and last page answer each other, your ending is earned.

💪 KEY IDEA

Endings are bought, not found: planted details, balanced evidence, and a narrator's tone are the currency, and the last paragraph is the purchase. Seven masters have shown you seven ways to pay. Choose yours, check the receipt, and finish your story.
