●
The conclusion runs 100 to 150 words. It says what changes if you are right. It does not repeat what you just said. Ask yourself: if a reader believed your thesis, what would they see differently?
MODEL CONCLUSION OPENING (GETTYSBURG AND THE SCARLET LETTER)
If a standard means nothing unless its makers accept it, then a reader should ask of any founding promise not what it says but who is willing to be measured by it. Lincoln answers by speaking as one of the people under test. Boston never does.

Assemble and revise
Put the pieces in order and test them
●
Put your paper together in Word in this order: introduction, bodies, counterargument, conclusion. Then work through these checks. Do each one on the real document.
REVISION CHECKS
  • Cover test. For every body paragraph, cover one text. If the point still stands, add the missing text into the claim.
  • Question and thesis. Your question is stated in the introduction, and your thesis answers it.
  • Quotations. Open the text and check every quotation word for word. Each sits inside a sentence of your own and is followed by what it shows.
  • Sources. You have four, in the required mix, each doing work. Every idea that is not yours is cited, including ideas you put in your own words.
  • Length. 1,200 to 1,500 words, not counting the Works Cited page.
QUICK CHECK · NOT GRADED
A paraphrase of a source's idea, with no quotation marks. Does it need a citation?
