Settle on four sources

●
Feeder 2 is an annotated bibliography. A bibliography lists sources. An annotated one says what each source is worth and what you will do with it. Building it now means that when you draft, you already know which source answers which part of your question. Start from the sources you found in The Pair.

2
Four questions for every source
Currency, relevance, authority, purpose
●
Every source gets the same four questions. Say what you found. Do not just say you looked.
1
Currency
When was this made, and does that matter here?
2
Relevance
Does it answer your question or a nearby one? Name the part it touches.
3
Authority
Who made it, and why believe them? Check outside the page.
4
Purpose
Why does it exist: to inform, persuade, sell, or recruit?
●
On currency: for a 1787 document, a 1950s scholarly article can be perfectly good. For a claim about what historians think now, it is not. Say which case you are in. On authority: a site's About page is written by the site. Open a new tab and search for the author or the organization on their own.
QUICK CHECK · NOT GRADED
What is the reliable way to check a source's authority?
3
What an annotation looks like
Four sentences, each with a job
●
An annotation is a full citation followed by three or four sentences. Here is one on a different topic. Copy the shape, not the content.
ONE ENTRY, ANNOTATED
Maier, Pauline. American Scripture: Making the Declaration of Independence. Knopf, 1997.
Maier, a historian at MIT, argues that the Declaration was a distillation of roughly ninety local declarations written across the colonies in the months before July 1776. This helps my question, because it means the grievances were already familiar complaints, which changes what the list was for. Authority: a book from a major publisher by a scholar of the Revolution, cited by other historians I found on JSTOR. Purpose: to inform, though she is arguing against the lone-genius version. I will use her where I explain why the grievances are written as a pattern.
●
Count the jobs. One sentence says what it argues. One says why it matters to the question. One says who made it and why. One says where it goes. None says the source was helpful.

Worth knowing. An AI tool is bad at bibliographies in one specific way: it makes citations that are formatted perfectly and refer to nothing. A real author, a real publication, a believable title, and an article that does not exist. Every entry above must be a source you opened. If you cannot produce it when asked, do not list it.
5
Find three meeting points
The ideas your body paragraphs will grow from
●
Your paper holds two texts at once. Before you draft, find three places where they meet. Each one can become a body paragraph in The Draft. A meeting point is an idea both texts speak to, even if they disagree about it.
MODEL MEETING POINT (GETTYSBURG AND THE SCARLET LETTER)
Idea: who stands inside the standard. The document: Lincoln says the war tests the nation, and he speaks as one of the people being tested. The story: Boston's leaders judge Hester by their law, and the man who shares her sin keeps his secret.
●
Write your own three. Describe each moment in your own words. You will find the exact words to quote in The Draft, and you will check them against the text.
