Choose a thread, not a topic
A thread is a question a text keeps asking
●
A topic says what a text contains. A thread is a question a text keeps asking and answers in its own way. You will follow one of four threads. The play touches all four, so any of them can work.
Justice
What is due to a person, and who decides it?
Loyalty
What do you owe someone when the truth is costly?
Family
What do households do to each other under pressure?
Duty
What does a role or a promise require, even when it hurts?
Why this thread?
One sentence. What in The Crucible made you pick it?

From theme to question
Four narrowing steps
●
A theme is a subject. A research question is something sources can settle. Each step below removes options, and the question at the bottom is the only one your source has to answer.

8
Pick an angle
Each angle points to a different kind of source
SALEM, 1692
How do the real events compare with the play? Look for scholarly articles, books, and reference entries on the trials.
THE 1950S HEARINGS
How did the period shape the play? Look for scholarly articles on McCarthyism and the arts, and period newspapers.
CRITICISM OF AN EARLIER TEXT
What do critics say about a text you will compare to the play? Look for scholarly articles on that text.

Write your research question
One sentence that ends in a question mark
A MODEL, ON A DIFFERENT THEME
Theme: honesty. Texts: Sir Gawain and the Green Knight, The Scarlet Letter, The Crucible.
Question: How do critics explain why Hester refuses to name the father of her child, and does the same reasoning explain the choices made by characters in the other two texts?
Your research question
Narrow enough to answer, open enough to argue. Use your theme and at least one of your texts.

Judge a source before you use it
The four-question credibility check
●
Feeder 1 asks for three candidate sources, each one you opened yourself. At least one is a scholarly source from JSTOR. The Research and JSTOR Access page in Start Here shows how to get in. Today you practice on your first source.
1question
CURRENCY
When was it written? Ideas about Salem and about the 1950s have changed since.
2question
RELEVANCE
Does it address your research question, or only the same topic?
3question
AUTHORITY
Who wrote it, and where was it published?
4question
PURPOSE
Is it written to inform, to argue, or to sell? A source can be accurate and still leave out evidence that cuts the other way.
QUICK CHECK · NOT GRADED

Project Feeder 1: Research Question and Sources, 15 points, due in Week 2

At least one of your three sources is a scholarly source from JSTOR. Which one?

PURPOSE

This step sets the theme, the question, and the first sources for the module project.

STEPS
  1. Choose one theme: justice, loyalty, family, or duty.
  2. Name three texts for the theme: The Crucible and at least two earlier course texts.
  3. Write one research question that is narrow enough to answer and open enough to argue.
  4. Find three candidate sources and open each one yourself.
  5. Write an MLA entry for each source.
  6. Write one line on each source’s credibility: one reason to trust it and one limit.
