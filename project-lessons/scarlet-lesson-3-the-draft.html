PURPOSE

Turn your thesis, three quoted moments, and researched source into a paragraph-by-paragraph plan for the 900–1,200 word essay.

THE SHAPE OF THE ESSAY

Introduction100–150Opening, context, thesis

Body 2200–250Claim, quoted moment, analysis
Body 3200–250Claim, quoted moment, analysis; researched source enters where it fits

STEPS
  1. State your thesis, revised from Feeder 2, in one or two sentences.
  2. Plan the introduction: the opening, the context, and where the thesis falls.
  3. Plan three body paragraphs: a claim, the quoted moment with its chapter number, and the point the analysis makes.
  4. Mark where your researched source enters and what it adds, with its MLA entry and in-text citation; the source supports your reading and does not replace it.
  5. Plan one counterclaim paragraph: the strongest opposing view and your response.
  6. Plan the conclusion: how the thesis answers the theme question.

A MODEL INTRODUCTION (ON A DIFFERENT CLAIM ABOUT PEARL)
Opening. A child who will not obey is a problem for a town that runs on rules.
Context. In The Scarlet Letter, the Puritans of Boston punish Hester Prynne in public, and her daughter Pearl grows up beside the punishment.
Thesis. Hawthorne shows that a town can punish a mother but cannot make her child obey its rules.
