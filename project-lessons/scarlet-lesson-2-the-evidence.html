The MLA entry and a two-sentence summary
Record the source so a reader could find it
●
MLA is the citation style this essay uses. An MLA entry lists the author, the title, where it was published, and the date, in a set order. Copy the details from the page itself. Here is the pattern for a journal article. The capital words are placeholders, not a real source.
THE PATTERN
Lastname, Firstname. "Title of the Article." Journal Name, vol. NUMBER, no. NUMBER, YEAR, pp. NUMBER-NUMBER. JSTOR, URL.
Your MLA entry
Follow the pattern. If a part does not apply to your source, leave it out.
Its argument in two sentences, in your own words
What does the writer claim, and what is the main reason? Do not copy a sentence from the source.
3
Four questions to ask of any source
One reason for each, specific to your source
●
A source can be real and still not be good for your essay. Ask four questions. Currency: when was it written, and does the date matter for this topic? Relevance: does it actually help with your prompt? Authority: who wrote it, and what makes them a reliable voice? Purpose: why was it written: to inform, to argue, or to sell something?

PURPOSE

The Source Note records one researched source for the essay and judges whether it can be trusted.

STEPS
  1. Find one source about Puritan New England that you opened yourself. A scholarly article from JSTOR is preferred.
  2. Write the source’s MLA entry.

  5. State your judgment: strong, usable with care, or weak.

A MODEL ENTRY · ON A DIFFERENT CLAIM
"It is probable that there was an idea of penance in this mode of occupation, and that she offered up a real sacrifice of enjoyment, in devoting so many hours to such rude handiwork."
Hawthorne, ch. 5 (Hester at her needle)
MODEL CLAIM, CITATION, AND PROOF
Claim. Hawthorne shows Hester choosing a way to serve her town while she is being punished by it.
Citation. (Hawthorne, ch. 5).
What it proves. The narrator says she "offered up a real sacrifice of enjoyment," so her sewing for the poor is not only a task given to her. It is something she gives.

PURPOSE

This step fixes the essay’s claim and the evidence behind it before drafting begins.

STEPS
  1. Write a one-sentence claim about The Scarlet Letter that a reader could dispute.
  2. Choose three moments from three different chapters (Chapters I to XIX). Copy each quotation exactly, one or two sentences long.
  3. Cite each quotation in MLA form, for example (Hawthorne, ch. 14).
  4. Under each quotation, write one sentence saying what it proves about the claim.
  5. Write two or three sentences on the Feeder 1 source: the idea it supplies, the paragraph it belongs in, and whether it supports, extends, or complicates the claim.
  6. Add MLA works-cited entries for the novel and the source.
ONE EVIDENCE ENTRY, FOR SHAPE · A SAMPLE ON A DIFFERENT CLAIM
Claim: Hawthorne treats revenge as a wound the avenger inflicts on himself.
Quotation: “A mortal man, with once a human heart, has become a fiend for his especial torment!” (Hawthorne, ch. 14).
Proof: Chillingworth admits that revenge has turned a man “with once a human heart” into a fiend, so the avenger is the one most changed by the revenge.
