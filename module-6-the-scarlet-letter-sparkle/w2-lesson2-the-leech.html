The Leech and His Patient

Chapters IX–XII
Header: Mary Hallock Foote, engraving for the 1878 edition.
Read aloud
Focus

A physician moves into the minister’s house to cure him. What is he really searching for, and what does the minister hide?

Objectives
  • Trace the physician’s search through five comparisons the narrator makes.
  • Compare what the congregation sees in Mr. Dimmesdale with what he knows of himself.
  • Explain how a confession can be true and still mislead.
  • Put the night on the scaffold in order and explain what Pearl asks of the minister.
  • Support a claim about hidden guilt with exact, cited quotations.

Before You Read

A doctor needs the truth to treat a patient. Is there anything a patient should be allowed to keep from a doctor? Give your answer and one reason. 3–4 sentences.
Your reading task
  1. Read Chapters IX–XII.
  2. Mark every comparison the narrator uses for the physician’s search, and every place where the minister’s public face and private life split apart. Select the line and choose Evidence.
  3. Note in the margin what each mark shows about the physician or the minister.
