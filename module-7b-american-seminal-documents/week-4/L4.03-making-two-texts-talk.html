Holding a Nation to Its Words
MODULE QUESTION
What happens to a promise a nation makes to itself?

Making a Document and a Story Talk to Each Other
Lesson
📖 FOCUS
Your paper puts one American document beside one work of literature you read this year. What does it actually mean to put two texts “in conversation”?
Module 5 distinguished stacking two sources from synthesizing them. Here the same distinction applies to two whole texts, from different centuries and different genres, made to answer the same question.
🎯 OBJECTIVES
1
I can build a question that two very different texts can both answer.
2
I can distinguish synthesis from summarizing two texts one after the other.
3
I can avoid the two most common failures: forcing a similarity, and comparing nothing.
📚 PART 1 · THE DIFFERENCE, IN TWO PARAGRAPHS
STACKING, THEN SYNTHESIS
Stacking: The Declaration argues that a government which violates rights may be replaced. In Sir Gawain and the Green Knight, Gawain breaks his promise by keeping the green girdle and is shamed for it. Both texts are about promises.
Two accurate summaries and a sentence that notices they share a word. Nothing here required both texts.

Synthesis: Gawain's court laughs at his broken oath and adopts the girdle as a badge, which resolves his failure by lowering the standard. The Declaration takes the opposite path: it insists the standard is fixed and the failure belongs to the King. Reading them together sharpens the real question — when a promise is broken, does a community protect itself by adjusting the promise, or by naming the breach?
Now each text is doing something the other cannot, and the third sentence could not exist without both.

The test

Cover one of your two texts with your hand. If your point survives, you have not synthesized — you have written about one text and mentioned another. A real synthesis collapses when either half is removed.

📚 PART 2 · FINDING YOUR PAIRING

These are starting points, not assignments. If you see a better pairing, take it — but be able to say what question the two texts both answer.

Literary textDocumentA question both can answer
Electra (Module 1)The Declaration of IndependenceWhen existing authority will not deliver justice, what makes taking action into your own hands legitimate rather than merely vengeful?
Sir Gawain and the Green Knight (Module 2)The Constitution and the Bill of RightsIs a promise strengthened or weakened by writing down exactly what happens when it is broken?
The Canterbury Tales (Module 3)Federalist No. 10When people's interests differ by their position in society, can a shared arrangement be trusted to be fair?
The Scarlet Letter (Module 6)Lincoln's Second InauguralCan a community judge a wrong honestly while admitting its own share in it?
The Machine Stops (Module 5)The Four FreedomsWhat is a person owed beyond being left alone — and can comfort be a form of unfreedom?

Two ways this goes wrong

Forcing the similarity. The most common failure. A student decides the two texts are “both about justice” and then spends five pages pressing them into a shape neither one has. The cure is to look for the place they disagree. Two texts that answer the same question differently give you an argument; two texts that agree give you a summary.

Comparing nothing. The other failure is a paper with a paragraph on the document, a paragraph on the novel, and a final paragraph announcing that they are similar. If your conclusion is where the comparison happens, the comparison has not happened. It belongs in every body paragraph.

📌 A SHAPE THAT WORKS
Each body paragraph makes one claim about your question and brings in both texts to support or complicate it. Not a paragraph per text — a paragraph per idea, with both texts inside it. If a paragraph mentions only one text, either it is doing background work or it is in the wrong paper.
✎ CHECK YOURSELF · PICK THE BEST ANSWER
1. Which sentence shows real synthesis?
2. You find your two texts agree completely. What should you do?
📓 PRACTICE

For the project:

▶
Name your two texts and write the question they will both answer, as a question.
▶
Write one sentence saying how they differ in answering it. If you cannot yet, your question is still too general.
▶
Apply the cover test to that sentence and record the result.
💪 KEY IDEA

Two texts in conversation are not two texts in the same paper. The test is simple and unforgiving: if your point survives when one of them is removed, you have not made it yet.
